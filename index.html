Redemption-Bounded Market Making over Shared Liquidity

Vault shares, liquid-staking tokens and yield-bearing dollars already tell you what they convert into. Tidewater quotes against that boundary instead of pool inventory — at the size actually requested, and adjusted for how long settlement really takes. It runs as a SwapVM program and settles through 1inch Aqua.

The mechanism

Three rules, and a refusal to guess

Bounds, not rates

An adapter returns the least you would get redeeming Q units, the most it would cost to replace them, and the capacity both numbers survive. convertToAssets() is an estimate, not a promise, and is never used as a bound.

Delay is priced

A queued exit commits capital for days. Quoting the nominal bound anyway means financing someone else's withdrawal queue for free, so the bound takes an explicit carry and hazard haircut before it becomes a ceiling.

Fail closed

Stale state, a missing permission, an unbounded settlement time, capacity exhausted between quote and fill — any of them produces no quote rather than an optimistic one. Uncertainty reverts.

Scope

What the guarantee does not cover

The result is narrow on purpose. Within a valid conversion path, its declared capacity and its declared settlement delay, carry-adjusted loss relative to redemption is bounded by a budget you set explicitly.

Outside those assumptions it covers nothing. Not issuer default, not a depeg of the reference asset itself, not governance seizure, not a withdrawal queue that runs longer than the adapter claimed. That last one is the exposure the corridor genuinely cannot bound, and the paper says so rather than pricing it away.

This is design work. Nothing is deployed.
